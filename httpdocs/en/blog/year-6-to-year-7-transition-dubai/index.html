Year 6 to Year 7 Dubai: The Secondary School Transition Guide

Year 6 Year 7 Dubai secondary school transition Dubai Year 7 entry Dubai after primary school Dubai Dubai secondary school applications
In this guide: When to apply for Year 7 secondary school in Dubai, which schools accept external applicants, what assessments are required (including CAT4), how to prepare your child and what the transition year actually looks like.

If your child is in a Dubai primary school — especially a primary-only school like GEMS Jumeirah Primary, Raffles International School Primary or Swiss International Scientific School — Year 7 planning needs to begin far earlier than most families expect. The transition from primary to secondary in Dubai is not automatic. It requires research, active applications, assessments and, for popular schools, being on a waiting list.

This guide explains exactly what is involved, when to act and how to match your child's profile to the right secondary school.

Why the Year 6–7 Transition Requires Early Action

Unlike many countries where secondary school places are allocated by local authority or catchment zone, Dubai secondary schools operate as independent private institutions. Each school sets its own admissions timeline, assessment process and capacity.

The most sought-after secondary schools — JESS Dubai, Kings' School Dubai, Brighton College and Jumeirah College — receive more applications than they have Year 7 places. Many families who begin applying in Year 6 discover the school is already full for their year group. The correct approach is to begin researching in Year 4 and applying seriously by Year 5.

The CAT4 Assessment: What to Expect

The CAT4 (Cognitive Abilities Test, Fourth Edition) is the standard admissions assessment at most Dubai British and IB secondary schools. It assesses four reasoning batteries:

  • Verbal Reasoning — vocabulary, verbal analogies, sentence completion
  • Quantitative Reasoning — number patterns, arithmetic sequences, data interpretation
  • Non-Verbal Reasoning — figure classification, matrices, pattern recognition
  • Spatial Ability — 2D/3D shape manipulation, figure rotation

The CAT4 is not a curriculum knowledge test. It measures reasoning potential, not what your child has been taught. This means children who perform well academically may not automatically score highly on CAT4, and vice versa. Read our full CAT4 guide and practice questions by battery for preparation guidance.

Schools typically use CAT4 scores to assess fit rather than to filter — a child with a moderate CAT4 score but strong academic records and a good interview may still be offered a place. Conversely, a high CAT4 score without supportive school reports rarely secures a place at a highly selective secondary.

The Academic Step-Up in Year 7

The academic demand in Year 7 is noticeably higher than Year 6, particularly in these areas:

  • Independent study: Year 7 students are expected to manage homework across multiple subjects without the degree of teacher scaffolding that primary schools provide
  • Subject specialisation: Instead of one or two main teachers, children have 8–12 subject specialists. The pace of each lesson increases
  • English writing demands: Extended analytical writing (not just creative or descriptive) begins in earnest in Year 7
  • Mathematics: The gap between primary and secondary mathematics widens significantly. Algebra, geometry proofs and data handling all accelerate

If your child is at the lower end of their Year 6 cohort academically, the Year 7 step-up is an important consideration. Some families use the transition summer to consolidate maths and English — not to get ahead, but to ensure the foundations are solid.

Preparing Your Child for the Year 6–7 Transition

Academic preparation

  • Begin CAT4 practice from Year 5 — not intensive drilling, but familiarisation with the format (see our CAT4 practice questions guide)
  • Ensure maths foundations (particularly arithmetic, fractions, basic algebra) are secure before Year 6 ends
  • Build extended writing stamina — Year 7 English requires longer structured responses than most Year 6 students are used to

Social and emotional preparation

  • The social transition is usually less difficult than parents fear. Children are resilient and actively motivated to make new friends at a new school
  • If your child is anxious about leaving primary friends, reassure them that many classmates from popular Dubai primaries end up at the same secondary school
  • Attend secondary school open days with your child in Year 4–5 — schools have informative events specifically designed for prospective Year 7 families

What If You Miss the Main Application Round?

If you are in Year 6 and have not yet applied to secondary schools, do not panic — but act immediately. Options include:

  • Contact schools directly to check for places or join their waiting list
  • Broaden your school list beyond the top-tier Outstanding schools — Very Good schools often have Year 7 places available mid-cycle
  • Apply for a January start instead of September if your timelines permit — some schools accept mid-year entries for Year 7
  • If you are relocating to Dubai mid-Year 6, a summer arrival gives you time to apply for September Year 7 entry during the reserve application rounds

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I start applying for Year 7 in Dubai?

Begin research in Year 4 and make formal applications in Year 5 (September–October). Most KHDA Outstanding secondary schools fill their Year 7 cohort 12–18 months before entry. Waiting until Year 6 significantly limits your choices at the most popular schools.

What tests are required for Year 7 entry in Dubai?

Almost all Dubai British-curriculum secondary schools use the CAT4 (Cognitive Abilities Test). Some schools also set their own English and Mathematics assessments or conduct interviews. Check each school's admissions page for their specific requirements.

Which Dubai secondary schools accept children from primary-only schools?

All major Dubai secondary schools accept external Year 7 applicants. Popular choices include JESS Dubai, Kings' School, Brighton College, Dubai British School Emirates Hills, Safa Community School and Jumeirah College. Each has its own assessment process and demand level.

Is the Year 6 to Year 7 transition hard for children?

The academic step-up is real — more subjects, more independent work, faster pace. The social adjustment is usually faster than parents expect. Most children settle socially within 6–8 weeks. Academic consolidation in the transition summer (maths and extended writing) is more useful than anything else you can do to prepare them.

Do I need to pay a deposit to hold a Year 7 place?

Yes. Most Dubai secondary schools require a non-refundable deposit (typically AED 2,000–5,000) to confirm acceptance of an offer. This is due within a specific deadline after the offer is made — usually 2–4 weeks. Missing this deadline risks losing the place.
