School Enrolment in France:
A Step-by-Step Guide for Turkish Families

Back to Blog
Summary

A guide to school enrollment in France for Turkish families — covering the centralized national curriculum, administrative steps, language considerations, and the choice between French public schools and international options.

France runs its education system centrally — the Ministry of National Education (Ministère de l'Éducation Nationale) sets the same curriculum nationwide. That's good news: wherever in France you live, the curriculum is the same, making school transfers straightforward. The harder parts are the French language barrier and the bureaucratic process — both of which this guide addresses directly.

Özlem Çimen — Note from the coach

When I work with families relocating to France, I notice the same pattern: students take longer to adapt to the French system than expected. The language barrier is harder than anticipated; the bureaucracy slower. I wrote this guide so families see the reality first — not the romanticised version.

Personal note: Özlem Çimen raised her own children in Lycée Français International de Rangoun (Yangon), Lycée français de Bakou, Lycée Français Pierre Loti d'Istanbul and Lycée Français Jean Mermoz de Dubai. This guide draws directly from lived experience.

The French School System: Structure and Stages

Ages 3–11

École Maternelle & Primaire

Nursery (maternelle) from age 3; primary school (primaire) from age 6. State schools are free. Compulsory education begins at 6.

Ages 11–15

Collège

4-year middle school. Same curriculum for all students. Ends with the Brevet exam. Foreign language choice during this period is significant.

Ages 15–18

Lycée & Baccalauréat

3-year high school. General (Général), Technological or Vocational Bac options. The Baccalauréat is the gateway to French university entry.

All ages

Lycée Français & International

500+ Lycées Français worldwide. Lycées Internationaux de Paris offer IB and international sections. The global network is a major advantage for mobile families.

Key advantage: France's Lycée Français network spans 500+ schools worldwide. A child who starts in a Lycée Français in France can transfer to any school in the network globally with minimal disruption — a significant advantage for frequently relocating families.

Class System: Counting Down

French class numbering runs in reverse — which surprises most non-French families. The higher the number in the name, the younger the year group:

5ème (Cinquième)Collège Year 2 — ages 12–13
4ème (Quatrième)Collège Year 3 — ages 13–14
3ème (Troisième)Collège final year + Brevet exam

French Language Preparation

French state schools teach in French. For students with a language barrier, specialised language support programmes called UPE2A (Unité Pédagogique pour Élèves Allophones Arrivants) are available. These programmes are designed to accelerate newly arrived students' adaptation to French — they are a support mechanism, not a penalty.

Warning: French is harder to learn quickly than many families expect — particularly compared to English. For children over 14, starting a French state school without any French is very difficult. I recommend reaching at least A2 level before enrolment.

Enrolment Process: 7 Steps

  1. 1
    Register at the Mairie (town hall) — After arriving in France, submit your residence declaration at the local town hall. This document is required for school enrolment.
  2. 2
    Identify your catchment school — In France, children must attend the school assigned to their residential address (carte scolaire). Switching sectors is possible under specific conditions.
  3. 3
    Apply at the Mairie or school — For primary, apply at the town hall. For middle and high school, apply directly to the school (Rectorat/DSDEN).
  4. 4
    UPE2A language assessment — If French is insufficient, your child will be directed to the UPE2A class first. This is designed to support, not delay.
  5. 5
    Year group placement — The school assigns a year group based on age and academic level. Children without French may be placed one year back — this is standard practice.
  6. 6
    Submit documents — Deliver required documents to the school administration. Use an accredited translator for documents requiring translation.
  7. 7
    Start intensive French — Begin Alliance Française or private lessons before the school start date. Early language preparation is the single most decisive factor in a smooth first year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is education free in France?

State schools are completely free. Small material contributions (approximately €100–€200/year) and school lunch fees (€3–€5/day) are the only costs. International schools charge €10,000–€25,000+ per year.

Paris, or another city?

Paris and the Île-de-France region offer the widest international school selection. Lyon, Bordeaux, Strasbourg and Nice also have strong state school infrastructure and solid international options.

Is the Baccalauréat recognised by universities internationally?

Yes. The French Baccalauréat is widely accepted by universities worldwide. It is particularly strong for entry to European and Canadian universities.

Are there English-medium school options in France?

Most French state schools teach in French. English-medium international schools operate in Paris, Lyon, Bordeaux and other major cities. Some Lycées Internationaux offer bilingual French-English sections. If English-medium schooling across a wider choice of European cities matters, see our Netherlands school guide →

How quickly do children learn French?

Research on full language immersion shows children aged 6–12 reach basic communication ability in 6–9 months. Academic French proficiency — the kind needed for school exams — typically takes 3–5 years.
