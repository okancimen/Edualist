School Enrolment in Italy:
A Step-by-Step Guide for Turkish Families

Back to Blog
Summary

A guide for Turkish families enrolling their children in Italian schools — covering the tiered school system, mandatory education requirements, key registration steps, and international school options in major cities.

Italy is as renowned for its historic education tradition as for its culture and art. For Turkish families moving to Milan, Rome, Florence or elsewhere in Italy, the school system looks very different at first. This guide walks through Italy's school structure, the enrolment process, and the practical details step by step.

Özlem Çimen — Note from the coach

When I was working with a family settling in Italy, I heard how long they spent just obtaining a Codice Fiscale. I realised quickly: what makes Italy hard is not the system itself — it's the information gap. Everything in this guide I've told families one-by-one. Now it's all in one place — save yourself the time.

Important: Italy's academic year begins in the first week of September. State school enrolment for the following year opens in January–February. Late applications risk losing a place.

The Italian School System: Stages by Age

Compulsory education in Italy covers ages 6–16. The system is divided into three main stages, each with its own school types.

Ages 6–11

Scuola Primaria

5-year primary school. State schools are free. Foreign children can enrol directly; language support availability varies by school.

Ages 11–14

Scuola Secondaria I

3-year middle school. Same curriculum for all students. Ends with the Licenza Media exam, which influences high school choice.

Ages 14–19

Liceo / Istituto Tecnico

High school level. Specialisations include Liceo Scientifico (sciences), Classico (humanities/Latin/Greek) and Linguistico (languages). Ends with the Maturità exam for university entry.

All ages

International School

IB, British or American curriculum. Common in Milan and Rome. English-medium instruction. Annual fees approximately €10,000–€25,000.

Italian Language Preparation

Italian state schools teach in Italian. However, the Italian school system tends to be welcoming to foreign students. Most state schools offer a laboratorio linguistico (language support programme) for students with limited Italian.

Good news: Italian is relatively straightforward to learn for children — particularly compared to German or French. Children aged 6–12 tend to adapt remarkably quickly. With patience through the first 3–6 months, integration is typically very successful.

Enrolment Process: 7 Steps

  1. 1
    Obtain a Codice Fiscale — Italy's tax identification number is required for all official processes. It can be obtained from an Agenzia delle Entrate office or the Italian consulate before arrival.
  2. 2
    Register your address (Residenza) — Submit a residence declaration at the local town hall (Comune). This step is mandatory before school enrolment.
  3. 3
    Research local school options — Italian state schools are assigned by residential area. The local school administration office (Ufficio Scolastico) can tell you which school you are zoned for.
  4. 4
    Pre-register January–February — Enrolment for the following academic year typically opens in January. Applications are made through the MIUR online portal.
  5. 5
    Academic placement assessment — The school will conduct a short assessment to determine the appropriate year group, testing Italian language and maths level.
  6. 6
    Submit documents — Deliver the required documents in person to the school administration. Use an accredited translator for documents requiring Italian translation.
  7. 7
    Plan language support and integration — Enrol in the laboratorio linguistico programme. Private Italian lessons (ripetizioni) also accelerate adaptation significantly.

Milan or Rome?

Milan — Italy's business capital, with the widest international school network. Higher cost of living, but strong educational quality and a large expat community.

Rome — A richer cultural and historical environment. State schools are generally strong, and the cost of living is slightly lower than Milan. A good balance of quality and affordability.

Practical tip: Private tutoring (ripetizioni) is a deeply embedded part of Italian culture. Finding a private Italian tutor for your child is easy and affordable. Two to three hours per week in the first term makes a very noticeable difference to adaptation speed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is education free in Italy?

State schools are free — only small administrative contributions and materials costs apply (approximately €100–€300/year).

Are Turkish diplomas recognised in Italy?

Yes — Turkish education documents are recognised in Italy. They must be submitted with an apostille stamp and an accredited Italian translation.

When is the Liceo choice made?

At the end of middle school, at age 14. This is a significant decision — your child's interests, language level and long-term goals should all be weighed together carefully.

Are there enough English-medium international school options in Italy?

International schools with English-medium instruction exist in Rome, Milan and Florence, but the choice is more limited than in some other European countries. City selection and school research should happen before committing to a move. If a wider English-medium European school network matters, see our Netherlands school guide →

What university options does the Italian system open?

Foreign students graduating from Italian state schools with a Diploma di Maturità can apply to most Italian and EU universities. Graduates with an IB or A-Level diploma have wider international university access.
