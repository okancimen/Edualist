Summary

Dubai International Academy (DIA) opened its Emirates Hills campus in 2005 and became the first school in the UAE to offer a complete IB continuum — a distinction it still holds today. With 2,757 students from over 85 nationalities, DIA runs seamless IB education from KG1 through Year 13, and is the only full-IB school in Dubai to hold an Outstanding KHDA rating. The 2026 IB Diploma average of 36.3 with a 100% pass rate, 1,477 university offers and 87% first-choice placements make a compelling academic case. Fees sit in Dubai's upper-mid premium range — significantly below Dubai College for secondary years, and much more accessible for primary-age children. This review combines parent feedback from four platforms, KHDA inspection data, and a coaching perspective from someone who advises families on school selection every day.

Coaching Perspective: Who Is DIA Right For?

In my advisory work, DIA Emirates Hills becomes the natural front-runner for families who want a single school from nursery to sixth form, within a coherent IB framework. This is particularly compelling for families arriving in Dubai with young children (under 11) who want to avoid the disruption of a primary-to-secondary school change.

Where I exercise more caution: students who struggle with IB's self-directed demands, families who prioritise Arabic language development, children who find very large school environments overwhelming, or students experiencing academic stress. For those profiles, Repton School Dubai (British curriculum with structured pastoral support) or GEMS Wellington (broad curriculum range) may fit better.

The Value of Continuity

The practical value of a single-school IB journey from KG1 to Year 13 is consistently underestimated. When curriculum language, assessment philosophy and school culture remain stable from age 3, children absorb the IB methodology rather than learning to perform it at exam time. This is structurally different from schools where students join IB at Year 11 with no MYP foundation.

Compared to Dubai College, which begins only at Year 7, DIA allows families to enrol at KG1 and graduate at Year 13 with no school changes — a significant logistical and emotional advantage for internationally mobile families.

Admissions and Fees: What to Budget

DIA is selectively admitting but not as restrictive as Dubai College. Key financial planning points:

  • Entrance test fee: AED 525 (non-refundable)
  • New enrolment fee: 10% of annual tuition (e.g. ~AED 6,373 for Year 7)
  • Re-enrolment fee: 5% of annual tuition each year
  • EAL support: AED 4,500/year (if applicable)
  • External tutoring: Budget for possible tutoring costs in MYP/DP years
  • Availability: Emirates Hills is near capacity — apply at least 12 months in advance
  • Alternative: Al Barsha campus (KHDA Very Good) if Emirates Hills is full

For a step-by-step guide on applying to Dubai schools, see our Dubai school application guide.

IB vs A-Level: Which Pathway?

DIA's IB DP and CP pathways suit families targeting universities in the US, Canada, Netherlands or globally-mobile careers where IB is widely recognised. If the goal is specifically UK universities via UCAS, both IB and A-Level work — but A-Level (available at Dubai College) may streamline UK applications. For a full comparison, see our IB vs A-Level guide for Dubai families.

Ozlem Cimen — Coach's Note

DIA's status as the UAE's first full IB continuum school is not just a marketing point — it represents 20 years of institutional learning about how to deliver IB effectively at every developmental stage. The 36.3 DP average on a cohort of 184 is significantly harder to sustain than the same average on 40 students. What I tell advisory clients: the real question is not whether DIA is a good school — it clearly is — but whether your child is the type of learner who thrives in the IB's inquiry-based, self-managed framework. I've worked with very able students who found IB's open-ended demands genuinely destabilising. If your child needs clear structure and defined performance metrics, have an honest conversation about that before committing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Dubai International Academy's KHDA rating?

The Emirates Hills campus is rated Outstanding by KHDA in both 2022–23 and 2023–24 — the only full IB continuum school in Dubai to hold this rating. Teaching quality is Outstanding across all phases; student wellbeing and inclusive education are Very Good; Arabic and Islamic Education are Acceptable in some phases.

How much are Dubai International Academy's annual fees?

2025–26 KHDA-approved fees: KG1 AED 43,946 / Years 7–9 AED 63,726 / Years 10–11 AED 70,818 / Years 12–13 AED 77,866. Entrance test AED 525, new enrolment fee 10% of tuition, re-enrolment 5% annually, EAL support AED 4,500/year.

What does 'full IB continuum' mean at DIA?

The complete IB programme from KG1 (age 3) through Year 13 (age 18): PYP, MYP, then DP or CP + BTEC Level 3. DIA was the first school in the UAE to offer this model in 2005 and remains Dubai's only Outstanding full-IB school.

What are DIA's IB Diploma results?

2026: School average 36.3/45 (global ~30.2), 100% pass, 67% scored 35+, 23.6% scored 40+, cohort of 184. MYP 2026 average 46 (global 37.48). PISA 2025 Maths 620 (Dubai avg 511). 2024: 1,477 university offers, 87% first choice, 178 Russell Group, $12.69M scholarships.

How do I apply to Dubai International Academy?

Apply online at diadubai.com; entrance test fee AED 525. New enrolment 10%, re-enrolment 5% of annual tuition. Apply at least 12 months ahead — the school is near capacity. If Emirates Hills is full, consider Al Barsha (KHDA Very Good).

How many campuses does DIA have?

Three: Emirates Hills (2005, Outstanding, ~2,757 students, 482,000 sq ft), Al Barsha (2018, Very Good) and Town Square (opened August 2026). Each holds a separate KHDA registration. This review covers Emirates Hills.

Is DIA good for international families new to IB?

Yes — especially for families arriving with children under 11 who start from PYP. Twenty years of IB delivery means the methodology is embedded in school culture, not bolted on. Mid-stream joiners (Year 7+) should allow a transition period; EAL support is available at AED 4,500/year. The CP + BTEC pathway is a strong option for students who prefer applied learning over the full Diploma.
