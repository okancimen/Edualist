Dubai College Review 2026: Fees, KHDA & Parent Reviews

Dubai College review Dubai College fees 2026 Dubai College KHDA Dubai A-Level school Dubai College parent reviews Dubai Outstanding school
Quick Summary — Dubai College:
  • KHDA: Outstanding — every inspection since 2010–11 (BSO 2024: Outstanding)
  • Curriculum: British (GCSE + A-Level; no IB)
  • Ages: 11–18 (Years 7–13; secondary only — no primary)
  • Location: Al Sufouh, Dubai (near Sheikh Zayed Road)
  • Annual fees: AED 97,415 – 110,305 + AED 30,000 refundable debenture
  • Students: ~1,094 (small and selective)
  • Teacher turnover: 7% (one of Dubai's lowest)
  • Parent rating: 4.0 / 5 (WhichSchoolAdvisor, 61 reviews)
  • Not-for-profit: Yes — all surplus reinvested into the school
Summary

Dubai College is one of Dubai's most established and selective British secondary schools: founded in 1978 under an Emiri decree, operating on a 19-acre Al Sufouh campus, and Outstanding in every KHDA inspection since 2010–11. In 2025, it set an all-time record with 74% A*-A at A-Level and consistently places in the global top 30 for Oxbridge admissions. But it is secondary-only, requires a competitive entrance exam, and carries Dubai's highest fee structure including a AED 30,000 debenture. This review aggregates parent feedback from four independent platforms, KHDA and BSO inspection data, and my own coaching perspective to give you an honest, complete picture.

Dubai College has been Outstanding in every KHDA inspection since 2010–11 — a consistency that is genuinely rare among Dubai's private schools. The 2024 BSO (British Schools Overseas) inspection independently confirmed Outstanding across all major domains.

Inspectors noted that "lessons are almost always of the highest quality," with "exceptionally strong leadership at all levels." The ALPS Value Added score is 1 — meaning students make progress in the global top 1% compared to peers with similar entry profiles.

KHDA inspection context: KHDA paused routine inspections for 2024–25 and 2025–26 (except for newly opened schools), with a return to inspections announced for 2026–27. The most recent KHDA inspection result therefore dates from 2023–24.

Fees 2025–26

PhaseAnnual Fee (AED)
Years 7–11 (ages 11–16)97,415
Years 12–13 / Sixth Form (ages 16–18)110,305

New students pay a refundable debenture of AED 30,000 on joining; this is returned (less any outstanding amounts) when the student leaves. An annual re-registration fee of 5% of annual tuition (~AED 4,870) is due at the start of Term 3 each year. Transport and private music tuition are available at additional cost.

Real cost calculation: For a new Year 7 joiner, the first year outlay is: AED 97,415 (tuition) + AED 30,000 (debenture, refundable) + ~AED 525 (application) = roughly AED 127,940 in year one. The debenture is held as a deposit and returned at the end of the student's time at the school — it is not a fee. Firmly in Dubai's ultra-premium tier, though the not-for-profit structure guarantees fees stay in the school rather than going to shareholders.

Campus & Facilities (19 Acres)

The Al Sufouh campus is among the best-resourced secondary school sites in Dubai:

  • SPACE Centre (2020): Sports hall, indoor swimming pool, fitness suites, dance studios
  • Jafar Centre (2024): Mathematics, Art and Computer Science departments; Harkness rooms, library, makerspace
  • New A Block (2026): Dedicated debating rooms and Model UN facilities
  • 21 science laboratories, three Design & Technology suites
  • 900-seat auditorium, 160-seat lecture theatre
  • Three drama studios + Music Centre
  • 320 individual Sixth Form study bays
  • Rugby, football and cricket pitches; four all-weather netball/tennis courts

The not-for-profit model is directly visible in the facilities: the Jafar Centre and SPACE Centre were both funded from school surpluses. No shareholder dividend means no capital leaving the school.

Parent Reviews: What Four Platforms Say

I aggregated feedback from WhichSchoolAdvisor (61 reviews, 4.0/5), SchoolsCompared (80/100), EdCare, and edarabia. Compared to GEMS Wellington, the review picture here is more homogeneous — fewer extreme positives and negatives, more consistent moderate-to-strong praise, with a specific set of concerns:

Positives — What parents say
  • Teacher quality and stability outstanding (7% turnover)
  • Academic outcomes and university guidance exceptional
  • Small school — students don't get lost
  • Performing arts, music and sport programmes are first-rate

  • Strong pastoral care and mentorship system
  • "My three very different children have all blossomed here"
  • "Expectations thoroughly surpassed — academically and on the sports field"
Concerns — What parents say
  • Competitive entrance exam: ~3 applicants per place
  • Secondary only — no primary feeder, requires transition planning
  • Parent communication satisfaction: 56% (below UAE average)
  • Competitive culture not right for all students
  • Arabic (First Language) instruction rated Acceptable by KHDA

Note on parent communication: Only 56% of parents are satisfied with how the school communicates — noticeably below the UAE average for Outstanding schools. Strong academic outcomes don't compensate for feeling out of the loop as a parent. This is worth raising directly in any school visit before you commit.

Coaching Perspective: Who Is This School Right For?

A parent quote summarises it best: "It's certainly not the right school for every child — but for the right child, it is exceptional." I recommend Dubai College to families whose child is academically ambitious, self-motivated, and independent. The 1:10 student-teacher ratio and 7% teacher turnover create genuine consistency and individual attention that larger schools struggle to match.

I am more cautious when: the child needs significant learning support (SEN provision is rated Very Good, not Outstanding), the family values a low-pressure nurturing culture, English proficiency is at an early stage, or primary-secondary continuity matters. For those profiles, GEMS Wellington or Repton School Dubai (which cover all ages) may suit better.

The Small School Advantage

1,094 students and a 1:10 teacher ratio is genuinely personal by Dubai standards. Add 7% teacher turnover — compared to GEMS Wellington's 22% — and the maths are clear: a child who joins in Year 7 is likely to still have the same teachers in Year 11. That consistency matters enormously for academic development and pastoral continuity.

Admissions & Entrance Exam

  • Entrance exam: Year 7 entry — English, Mathematics and reasoning tests; Year 9+ — prior exam results and academic references also assessed
  • Competition: ~3 applications per place offered
  • Debenture: AED 30,000 — refundable when student leaves
  • Re-registration fee: 5% of annual tuition (~AED 4,870) paid every year
  • Timing: Apply at least 12 months in advance; submit to at least 3 schools simultaneously

For full step-by-step application guidance, see our Dubai school application guide and Dubai school waiting list guide.

Notes for Expat Families

Dubai College is an excellent fit for expat families with an academically strong child — but a few things to be clear on before applying:

  • English proficiency is critical: EAL support is limited; the entrance exam already filters for this. A child arriving with weak English is unlikely to pass the entry test.
  • GCSE + A-Level, not IB: Families used to IB-based international schools will find the pathway different. GCSE is the norm across UK-curriculum Dubai schools, but the post-16 choice (A-Level vs IB) matters for university applications.
  • Secondary only — plan your primary: The school starts at Year 7. A child joining at 11 needs a strong primary foundation beforehand, ideally in the same UK-curriculum system.
  • University target matters: A-Level is the gold standard for UK university applications via UCAS. For US-focused families, Dubai College's track record is strong (7 Ivy League places in 2024), but A-Level requires more careful positioning than IB in the US admissions process.

The school is in Al Sufouh, near Sheikh Zayed Road — accessible from Dubai Marina, JBR and Jumeirah, though morning traffic is a real planning factor.

Özlem Çimen — Coach's note

Dubai College has a special place in my consulting work. The not-for-profit model, the 7% teacher turnover, the ALPS Value Added score of 1 — these aren't surface statistics. This school genuinely invests in its students. But the question I ask every family considering it is: where is your child going to secondary at age 11, and is that plan solid? The secondary-only structure is the most commonly overlooked planning gap. If you're moving to Dubai with a 9-year-old, the Dubai College question starts now, not in two years.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Dubai College's KHDA rating?

Outstanding — every inspection since 2010–11. The most recent BSO inspection (2024) also rated Outstanding across all major domains. English, Mathematics and Science are Outstanding; Arabic (First Language) is Acceptable.

How much are the fees at Dubai College?

Annual fees for 2025–26: AED 97,415 (Years 7–11), AED 110,305 (Years 12–13). New students also pay a AED 30,000 refundable debenture, returned when the student leaves. Annual re-registration fee of ~AED 4,870 applies each year.

How do you get into Dubai College?

Via competitive entrance exam: Year 7 — English, Mathematics and reasoning tests; Year 9+ — prior results and references also assessed. Approximately 3 applicants per place. Apply at least 12 months in advance.

What curriculum does Dubai College offer?

UK National Curriculum → GCSE (Years 10–11) → A-Level (Years 12–13). No IB Diploma offered. Notable options include Latin, Philosophy, Further Mathematics, Triple Science and Politics.

What are Dubai College's A-Level results?

2025: 74% A*-A (all-time record), 93.6% A*-B (all-time record), 35.5% A*. Nine Oxbridge offers in 2025; consistently ranks in the global top 30 for Oxbridge admissions. GCSE 2024 average grade 8.4; 95% of entries graded 9–7.

What do parents say about Dubai College?

4.0/5 on WhichSchoolAdvisor (61 reviews), 80/100 on SchoolsCompared. Praised for teacher stability, academic outcomes, pastoral care and the not-for-profit ethos. Main concerns: parent communication (56% satisfied, below UAE average), competitive entry, secondary-only structure, and no IB/BTEC pathway.

Is Dubai College right for my child?

Excellent for academically ambitious, self-motivated students. Not the right fit for those needing significant SEN support, preferring a lower-pressure environment, or at an early stage of English acquisition. The school covers secondary only (Years 7–13) — plan your child's primary separately.
