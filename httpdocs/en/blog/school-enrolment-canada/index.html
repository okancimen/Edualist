School Enrolment in Canada: Step-by-Step Guide for Turkish Families

Özlem Çimen — Note from the coach

My close familiarity with Canada came through working with a family who relocated there. When I returned, I realised just how good Canadian public schools actually are — and how much smoother the transition is for families who understand the system in advance. This guide is my attempt to show you the surprises before they arrive.

Canada's key advantage: All public schools are high-quality and completely free once you meet the residency requirement. Private school costs are significantly lower than in other destination countries. A genuinely multicultural environment means families integrate faster than almost anywhere else.

Canada's Three Main School Systems

Canada is a federal country, so each province runs its own education system with its own school boards. The provinces most popular with Turkish families are Ontario, British Columbia (BC) and — to a lesser extent — Quebec.

🇨🇦
Ontario (GTA)

Highest concentration of Turkish families. Elementary (K–6), Middle (7–8), High (9–12). Both English and French immersion streams available.

More flexible curriculum. Large international family population due to Pacific location. Nature-centred education options.

Enrolment Process: 7 Steps

1
Obtain Proof of Residency — A lease agreement or utility bill (electricity, water). This is mandatory for school registration.
2
Apply for a SIN (Social Insurance Number) — Required before school registration can be completed.
3
Get your birth certificate translated — A notarised English translation of the original Turkish document is required.
4
Verify vaccination records — Turkish vaccination records are generally recognised, but may require a notarised English translation.
5
Register with the local school board — Log in to the TDSB, Peel or VSB online portal and complete the application.
6
Academic placement assessment — The school assigns a grade level based on age and an academic assessment.
7
Attend orientation — Most schools offer an orientation session in late August. Highly recommended for families new to the Canadian system.

Language Barrier and ESL Support

One of the strongest advantages of Canadian public schools is the ESL (English as a Second Language) and EAL (English as an Additional Language) programme built into most schools. Quebec is the exception — French immersion is the default there, creating a much heavier language burden than Ontario or BC.

Practical tip: Begin intensive English preparation with your child before the move. Language adaptation becomes significantly harder after age 14. Online language courses during the summer months can make a meaningful difference to the first year of school.

Application Timeline

June–July: Prepare proof of residency documentation
August: SIN application, birth certificate translation, vaccination record check
September (immediately on arrival): Register via TDSB / Peel / VSB portal
September (first week): School orientation — compulsory or strongly recommended

Which City Is Right for Your Family?

The city you choose in Canada directly shapes your school options. Each major city has distinct advantages and constraints for Turkish families.

Toronto (GTA — including Mississauga and Brampton): Home to Canada's largest Turkish community. The richest options for Islamic schools, Turkish supermarkets, mosques and social networks. Strong school boards include TDSB and Peel District. The trade-off: Toronto has the highest cost of living in Canada, particularly for rent.

Vancouver (BC): A smaller but tight-knit Turkish community. Nature-focused lifestyle and mild climate attract many families. BC's curriculum is slightly more flexible than Ontario's, and French Immersion options are strong. International families are well-represented, so children adapt quickly.

Montreal (Quebec): The lowest cost of living, but the highest language barrier. The public school system is largely in French; English-medium options are limited. If English-language education is the priority, Montreal deserves careful consideration before committing.

Choosing your city — four factors to weigh: Your child's age and English level; your family's social needs (Turkish community, mosque, Turkish grocery); your rent and cost-of-living tolerance; your employer's location. When these four are evaluated together, the right city usually becomes clear quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreign students attend Canadian public schools for free?

Families with Permanent Residency (PR) or certain visa types can access public schools at no cost. Children arriving on a student visa typically pay CAD $10,000–$14,000 per year in school fees.

What is the most important difference between Ontario and BC school systems?

Ontario operates a 12-year system leading to the OSSD (Ontario Secondary School Diploma). BC has a similar structure but places stronger emphasis on French Immersion options. Both provinces have strong IB-authorised schools.

What documents are needed for school registration in Canada?

Passport or birth certificate, proof of address, vaccination records, and a notarised English translation of your child's previous school transcript.

When does the Canadian school year start?

The school year typically begins in the first week of September and ends in late June. Some schools use a trimester system. A January mid-year entry is available at some schools.

Which Canadian city is best for Turkish families?

Toronto (including Mississauga and Brampton) hosts Canada's largest Turkish community — strongest for Islamic schools, Turkish groceries and social networks. Vancouver offers a smaller but cohesive Turkish community alongside a nature-focused lifestyle that appeals to many families.
