Summary

Families relocating to Dubai from abroad make the same five school-selection mistakes, over and over. I hear them in almost every consulting call — usually after the damage is done. This guide names each mistake clearly and gives you a concrete action step to avoid it.

5 Mistakes Expat Families Make
When Choosing a Dubai School

Dubai school selection mistakes how to choose an international school in Dubai KHDA school selection criteria Dubai school application tips expat families Dubai school guide

Over the years I have worked with hundreds of families. Families relocating to Dubai from Turkey, Europe, the US and beyond. And I hear the same mistakes — from families who have never met each other — over and over again.

I am not writing this to judge anyone. These mistakes are understandable: nobody gives you a manual on the Dubai school system before you arrive. But some are avoidable, and avoiding them makes an enormous difference.

Quick list: (1) Applying "once we arrive"  ·  (2) Choosing by WhatsApp group consensus  ·  (3) Treating the KHDA rating as the only criterion  ·  (4) Skipping the curriculum decision  ·  (5) Making location an afterthought

Mistake 1 — Applying "Once We Arrive"

I hear some version of this sentence at least once a week. The family has decided to move to Dubai, signed a work contract, sorted out the apartment — and school research is in the "we'll handle it when we get there" pile.

KHDA Outstanding schools have waiting lists of 12 to 24 months. JESS, DIA, Repton, GEMS Wellington — the picture is the same across all of them. If you say "we'll sort it when we arrive," your child will almost certainly start at a Very Good or Good school, and will then wait years on a list for the Outstanding school you actually want.

Action step: Start researching schools not on the day you decide to move, but in the same month you decide to move. If you are relocating in eight months, you may already be too late for Outstanding schools — but you are still in good time for Very Good ones.

Mistake 2 — Choosing by WhatsApp Group Consensus

The expat community in Dubai is active and generous. WhatsApp groups, Facebook pages, Instagram comments — everyone is asking for and giving school recommendations. And usually the same handful of names come up: "JESS is wonderful," "DIA is amazing," "check out Nord Anglia."

These recommendations are not wrong. But they can mislead you. The family recommending a school has a child with a different profile, a different learning style and different university ambitions than yours. "It worked brilliantly for us" guarantees nothing for your child.

Beyond that, certain schools become disproportionately popular within specific expat communities. When a school is the top choice of many families from the same background, its waiting list grows disproportionately — and it becomes a place everyone wants but fewer people can actually access.

Action step: Listen to recommendations, but build your own shortlist. Start from your child: their learning style, strengths, English level and university goals. Then filter schools against that profile. Community pressure is noise; profile match is signal.

Mistake 3 — Treating the KHDA Rating as the Only Criterion

KHDA Outstanding = good school. That is true. But KHDA Outstanding = right school for my child. That is not true.

KHDA inspections look at four categories: student achievement, quality of teaching, curriculum and personal development, leadership. These matter. But they do not answer questions like:

  • How strong is the EAL (English as an Additional Language) support? — Critical for families relocating from non-English speaking countries.
  • What are the class sizes? How much one-to-one attention does a student get?
  • Is teacher turnover high? (Outstanding rating but a different teacher every year)
  • Does the campus culture match your child's personality?
  • Does the extracurricular offering support your child's actual interests?

Read the KHDA report — but understand that the rating is not the whole picture.

Action step: Read the full inspection reports on the KHDA website. Pay particular attention to the "Areas for improvement" section. Attend open days and ask directly about EAL provision, class sizes and teacher stability.

Mistake 4 — Skipping the Curriculum Decision

"International school" immediately brings to mind English-language education. That is accurate — but it is not enough. Dubai has three main curriculum systems: IB, A-Level and American AP. The difference between them is not just content — it is a fundamentally different way of thinking.

IB (International Baccalaureate): Analytical thinking, self-management, project-based learning. Inquiry, not memorisation, is at the centre. Students coming from highly exam-focused national systems often experience a genuine systemic shock in Year 1 of IB. Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge, simultaneous Internal Assessments — serious preparation is essential before starting.

A-Level: Fewer subjects, much deeper. Students concentrate on 3–4 subjects over two years. Ideal for children targeting UK and Commonwealth universities who have decided on a field early. It can feel more familiar at first glance, but the question styles are very different.

American AP: A broad course load, a continuous assessment cycle and SAT/ACT requirements. Best for students targeting US universities.

Action step: Choose the curriculum first, then build your school shortlist. University ambitions and learning style should drive this decision — not the school name.

Mistake 5 — Making Location an Afterthought

The family chooses a school, then looks for a home. This sequence is a major problem in Dubai.

Dubai's Outstanding schools are scattered across the city: DIA is in Emirates Hills, JESS is in Umm Suqeim, Repton is in Nad Al Sheba, GEMS Wellington is in Al Sufouh, Nord Anglia is in Jumeirah Park. A poor neighbourhood choice means 40–60 minutes of commuting for your child every day. Both ways. Five days a week.

Most families who realise this mistake realise it after signing a lease.

Action step: Finalise your school shortlist before you look for a home. Then research neighbourhoods within 15–20 minutes of those schools. School and home decisions must be made together.

A 5-Step Framework for Getting It Right

Here is a clear process to put against each of those mistakes:

  1. Define your child's profile. Academic strengths, learning style, EAL need, university goals. Do not skip this step.
  2. Choose the curriculum. IB, A-Level or American — this decision comes before any school name.
  3. Fix your neighbourhood and maximum commute. If 20 minutes is your limit, establish that clearly from the start.
  4. Filter with KHDA Outstanding + curriculum + location to get 5–7 schools. Read the full KHDA reports, attend open days.
  5. Apply to all of them in parallel. Never rely on a single school. Apply to Outstanding schools as early as possible; Very Good and Good schools 6–9 months ahead.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common mistake when choosing a school in Dubai?

Applying too late. KHDA Outstanding schools have 12–24 month waiting lists — JESS, DIA, Repton, GEMS Wellington all face the same situation. Starting your search only after arrival almost guarantees you will begin at a Very Good or Good school while waiting years for your preferred Outstanding option.

Is a KHDA Outstanding rating enough to choose a school?

No. KHDA Outstanding tells you the school performs well across inspection categories — but not whether it fits your child. EAL support quality, class sizes, teacher turnover and campus culture are not visible in KHDA reports. Outstanding + right fit for your child = the right school.

IB, A-Level or American curriculum — which should I choose?

IB requires analytical thinking and self-management — skills that highly structured national curriculums don't always build directly, so the transition can be demanding. A-Level is subject-focused and deep — ideal for children targeting UK or Commonwealth universities. American AP requires a broad course load and SAT/ACT. The choice should be driven by your child's learning style, university target and English level.

Why does location matter so much when choosing a Dubai school?

A poor neighbourhood choice means 40–60 minutes of daily commuting for your child. DIA in Emirates Hills can mean an 80-minute daily round trip from JBR. Make your school shortlist first, then research neighbourhoods within 15–20 minutes of those schools. School and home decisions must be made together.

Which schools do expat families in Dubai most commonly choose?

JESS, Nord Anglia, DIA and Repton are the most frequently mentioned names in expat communities. But this is partly a herd effect. Profile match, application timing and location should come before community pressure when building your shortlist.
