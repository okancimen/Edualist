From Turkish Private Schools to Dubai International Schools 2026: Documents, CAT4 & Interviews

Quick Answer — What Documents Do You Need? Passport, visa, last 2 years' report cards (certified English translation), Transfer Certificate (authenticated via School → MEB → Apostille → UAE Consulate chain) and vaccination record. Start the process at least 2–3 months before your move — late starters miss application windows.

For families with children, education planning sits at the top of every Dubai relocation list. Moving from Turkish private schools to Dubai international schools is an exciting step — but the registration process, paperwork and interviews can look complex. Here is everything you need to know, laid out clearly.

Important: Document processes take time. Start at least 2–3 months before your move to Dubai. Families who start late miss application deadlines.

1. Which Documents Do Dubai Schools Require?

All private schools in Dubai are regulated by KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority), which means enrolment standards are strict. Beyond the basic application documents (passport, visa, passport photo), the two most critical education documents are:

Required Education Documents

Last 2 Years' Report Cards: Your child's report cards must be translated into English by a certified sworn translator.
Transfer Certificate (School Leaving Certificate): Every student enrolling in a Dubai school from abroad must present this document. It officially confirms which school your child left, which year group they were in, and that they are eligible to continue their education.

2. The Document Authentication Chain

Official school documents from Turkey — especially the Transfer Certificate — must pass through a specific authentication chain before they are valid in Dubai. This takes time, so starting before your move is essential:

The 4-Step Authentication Chain

Step 1 — School Approval: The document must be written on the school's official headed paper and signed with a wet signature and stamp by the school principal.
Step 2 — MEB Approval: The document must be certified by the District or Provincial National Education Directorate (İlçe/İl Milli Eğitim Müdürlüğü).
Step 3 — Apostille: An Apostille / legalisation stamp from the Turkish Ministry of Foreign Affairs is required.
Step 4 — UAE Consulate Approval: Finally, the document must be endorsed by the UAE Embassy or Consulate in Turkey.
Remember: All documents must be translated from Turkish into English or Arabic by a sworn translator before or alongside this authentication chain.

3. What Is the CAT4 Test?

Most British, American and IB-curriculum schools in Dubai use the CAT4 (Cognitive Abilities Test 4) as part of their admissions assessment.

CAT4 does not measure your child's accumulated knowledge or memory — it measures potential, reasoning ability and learning style. The test has four sections:

CAT4 Sections

Verbal Reasoning: Relationships between words and logical connections.
Quantitative Reasoning: Numerical patterns and mathematical logic.
Non-Verbal Reasoning: Problem-solving using shapes and visual patterns.
Spatial Ability: Three-dimensional thinking and mentally rotating shapes.

It is often said that "you can't study for CAT4" — but becoming familiar with the question formats and the logic of the test significantly reduces exam-day stress, especially for children sitting a test in a second language. Practising with sample questions is genuinely useful.

Practical advice: CAT4 is conducted in English. Your child's familiarity with test instruction vocabulary — recognising what each question type is asking — directly affects their score.

4. Primary and Middle School Assessment Processes

Assessment processes at international schools vary significantly by age group:

Primary School

Assessments are predominantly play-based.
Rather than academic knowledge, the school observes the child's social adjustment and ability to follow instructions.
Basic literacy and numeracy awareness are assessed.

Middle School

The CAT4 assessment is typically introduced at this level.
Interviews with the admissions or guidance team assess the student's interests, hobbies and ability to express themselves.
Adaptation capacity and potential to settle into a new environment are key factors.

5. How Should Secondary Students Prepare for Interviews?

Secondary transitions are far more strategic because students are stepping into intensive programmes like IB Diploma, A-Level or AP.

A critical point: in the AP system, course selections begin in Year 9. This means secondary interview candidates are expected to be able to articulate their academic goals and plans with some clarity, even at an early stage.

Secondary Interview Preparation Steps

Portfolio Presentation: Clubs in Turkey, sports achievements, MUN (Model United Nations) experience and social responsibility projects should all be highlighted in the interview.
Autonomy: International schools expect students to take ownership of their own learning. Demonstrating this maturity in the interview is essential.
Realistic Goals: Knowing which curriculum (IB, AP, A-Level) is the right fit and discussing it confidently with the school significantly increases admission chances.
Özlem Çimen — Coach's note

The most common mistake I see in secondary interviews: the student is not prepared for "which university are you thinking of?" Answering "I don't know" to this question meaningfully reduces admission chances. Even if no firm decision has been made, a clear directional answer — "I'm considering engineering in the US" — is what's needed.

6. Overcoming the Language Barrier

Transitioning to a fully English-medium education system is the biggest concern most families bring to me. But managing this process is less daunting than it looks:

3 Ways to Bridge the Language Gap

EAL / ELL Support: Almost all international schools in Dubai have an EAL (English as an Additional Language) department. Students receive additional English language support during school hours, matched to their current level.
Join Social Activities: Language barriers are broken on the pitch, not at the desk. Joining a sport, music group or arts club at school is the fastest route to learning English naturally through socialising.
Summer Preparation: The summer before the transition is the ideal time to shift reading habits into English and build vocabulary before school starts.
