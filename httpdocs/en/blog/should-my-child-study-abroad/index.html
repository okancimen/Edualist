Should I Send My Child to School Abroad?

Back to Blog
Summary

A practical framework to help Turkish families decide whether to pursue international education abroad — moving beyond the romanticized view to address real benefits, challenges, and the right questions to ask.

I've heard this question from hundreds of Turkish families over the years — in WhatsApp groups, over coffee in Dubai, from mothers calling from Singapore, from fathers who just moved to London. And every time I say the same thing: there is no right or wrong answer to "should I send my child abroad?" But there are good questions to ask.

According to TÜİK, more than 5.5 million Turkish citizens now live outside Turkey — and recent migration data show the fastest-growing segment is skilled professionals aged 25–44. International schooling is no longer a niche choice; it is an everyday reality for millions of Turkish families. This guide cuts through the romanticism and gets to the truth.

Note from Özlem Çimen

I made this decision myself. When we moved abroad with young children, there were tears — including mine. But today, watching my children grow up across different countries and cultures, I would make that decision again every time. One thing I've learned from my own experience and hundreds of coaching sessions: the families who navigate this best are the ones who ask the hardest questions of themselves before they pack a suitcase.

Before you read on: This decision is not only about education quality. Your child's personality, your family's financial resources, your motivation for moving and your long-term plans are equally decisive factors.

What International Schooling Really Gives You — and the Honest Challenges

Search "international school advantages" and you'll find glossy success stories. Here is the unvarnished version.

What it genuinely delivers

  • Multilingual fluency — the most concrete gain. Research consistently shows children exposed to a second language early develop measurable advantages in cognitive flexibility, problem-solving and multi-perspective thinking.
  • Global university access — IB Diploma and A-Level open doors not just in the UK or US but at leading universities worldwide.
  • Cultural intelligence, global network, adaptability — these don't show up on a CV; they show up in character.

What is genuinely hard

  • Language barrier and adjustment period — typically 6–18 months of pressure on both academic and social fronts.
  • Social network disruption and loneliness risk — especially significant for children who move during adolescence.
  • High financial burden — school fees, cost of living, language courses and preparation costs must all be factored together.
  • Identity complexity — "not fully from there, not fully from here" is one of the most frequently cited experiences of Turkish children raised abroad.
  • Equivalency challenges on return — if the family eventually moves back to Turkey, foreign qualifications require a formal recognition process.

✦ Strengths

  • Multilingual fluency and global mindset
  • Strong school profile via IB or A-Level
  • Cultural intelligence and adaptability
  • Global friendships and professional network
  • Independence and self-confidence
  • Access to world universities
  • Critical-thinking-focused education

◆ Real Challenges

  • Language barrier (6–18 months)
  • Social network disruption and loneliness
  • High cost — school + living expenses
  • Identity complexity: "between two worlds"
  • Equivalency issues if returning to Turkey
  • Increased parental stress
  • Distance from extended family and support network

The Age Factor: When Is the Right Time?

The single most common question I receive: "when should we start?" The answer is both neurobiological and practical — age shapes both language adaptation and social-emotional adjustment.

The neuroscience: Research shows children up to age 10 can acquire a second language to near-native level and without an accent. After that, language learning is still entirely possible — but it demands significantly more effort, time and emotional resilience. This table is not a verdict; I've seen teenagers move at 16 and thrive. But a 16-year-old transition requires a fundamentally different and more intensive support system than a move at age 7.

Will Turkish Be Lost? The Heritage Language Question

This is one of the most frequent concerns I hear, and rightly so. The short answer: yes, there is a risk. Children who move abroad between ages 5–8 and enter environments where English is spoken at home too can lose Turkish as their dominant language.

See this as a manageable risk, not a catastrophe. Conscious and consistent Turkish at home, reading Turkish books together, keeping a live connection to Turkish culture — these practices substantially reduce language loss risk. I always spoke Turkish with my children; it wasn't a rule, it was a choice. And that choice is today the foundation of both their identity and one of their strongest assets.

Family Profile: Is This Decision Right for You?

🟢 International schooling is likely a strong option if:
Your child is curious and has a high capacity to adapt · Your family is already relocating abroad or has long-term international plans · Financial preparation is in place · University goals are global
🟡 Careful evaluation needed if:
Your child has a very strong social network and may resist leaving · Your transition support system isn't yet established · The financial picture is uncertain · Language preparation hasn't started
🔴 Sending a child abroad alone (under 14):
Separating children under 14 from their parents for international schooling carries significant psychological risks. This decision requires extremely careful evaluation — the child's personality and emotional maturity are decisive. Moving as a family is always the safer scenario.

Curriculum Choice: IB, A-Level or AP?

A practical guide to the most common international curricula:

  • IB (International Baccalaureate) — the widest university acceptance network globally. The strongest passport to prestigious universities in Europe, North America and Asia. Research and critical thinking focused.
  • A-Level — the UK standard. First choice for UK and Commonwealth university applications. Deep but narrow — strong subject focus, less breadth.
  • AP (Advanced Placement) — integrated with the US university system. A strong option for families targeting American higher education.

On returning to Turkey: YÖK recognises IB, IGCSE/A-Level and AP qualifications and conducts equivalency assessments for foreign high school diplomas, giving qualifying graduates the right to university placement.

Decision Checklist

Before you decide, honestly answer these:
  • Have you had an honest, age-appropriate conversation with your child?
  • Is your school target and curriculum preference clear?
  • Have you calculated the full financial picture — school fees + living costs + preparation?
  • Is a language preparation plan in place?
  • Have you researched the return scenario and equivalency process?
  • Have you assessed your child's social and emotional readiness?
  • Is there a plan for maintaining Turkish language and cultural identity?

The most common mistake: treating international schooling as a prestige signal. Decisions driven by "the neighbour's child studies in London" typically cause significant stress within 2–3 years — because your child's real needs, personality and your family's actual life plan are entirely different from anyone else's. The right decision is your decision — made consciously, for your child and your family.

What is the best age to move abroad with children for schooling?

Ages 3–10 offer the easiest language and social adaptation. Children in this window typically reach near-native English fluency within 12–18 months. Moves at 10–13 are very manageable with proper planning. Adolescent transitions (14+) require significantly more preparation, emotional support and typically a structured language course before the move.

How do I choose between IB, A-Level and AP?

Start with your target university region: UK/Commonwealth → A-Level; global or European universities → IB; US universities → AP or IB. Then consider your child's learning style — IB is broader and more research-focused; A-Level is deeper in fewer subjects. If you're genuinely undecided, IB is the most flexible choice because it's accepted everywhere.

Will my child lose their Turkish?

There is a real risk, particularly for children aged 5–8 moving into English-dominant environments. The mitigation is conscious effort: speak Turkish at home consistently, read Turkish books together, maintain cultural connections. Families who make this a deliberate practice rather than an afterthought overwhelmingly preserve their children's Turkish.

What are the real total costs of international schooling?

School fees vary widely by destination: Dubai international schools run AED 40,000–120,000/year; UK independent schools £15,000–50,000/year; US private schools $25,000–60,000/year. Add cost of living, language preparation, uniform, activities and boarding — the full-year budget is typically 40–60% higher than tuition alone. See our International Education Cost Comparison →

If we return to Turkey, will the foreign diploma be recognised?

Yes — YÖK conducts equivalency assessments for foreign high school diplomas and recognises IB, IGCSE, A-Level and AP qualifications. Qualifying students receive the right to university placement in Turkey. Start the equivalency process as early as possible; the paperwork takes time. Keep certified translations of all school documents throughout your time abroad.
