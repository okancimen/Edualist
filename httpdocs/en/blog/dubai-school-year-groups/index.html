Dubai School Year Groups Explained: Which Year Does My Child Join?

Dubai school year groups which year group Dubai Dubai school age guide year group by age Dubai Dubai school enrollment age
Quick answer:
  • Year 1 start age: 5 years old by 1 September
  • Age cutoff: 1 September (most British-curriculum schools)
  • Placement is based on: birth date + previous school report; placement test for Year 7+
  • Borderline cases: CAT4 or academic assessment result can tip the decision
  • Highest year group: Year 13 (A-Level / IB Diploma final year)
Summary: Dubai's international schools — the vast majority of which follow a British or IB framework — use the British "Year group" system. This runs from Reception (age 4–5) through Year 13 (age 17–18). If you're relocating from a country using a different grade system, one year of offset is common and the 1 September age cutoff is the key variable. This guide maps out every year group by age, explains the placement process and covers what happens in borderline situations.

How the British Year Group System Works

Dubai's KHDA-regulated schools predominantly use British National Curriculum or IB programme structures. Both use "Year group" terminology:

  • Reception (Rec): Ages 4–5 — the foundation year before formal schooling
  • Year 1 – Year 6: Primary school, ages 5–11
  • Year 7 – Year 11: Secondary school, ages 11–16 (IGCSE in Years 10–11)
  • Year 12 – Year 13: Sixth Form / IB Diploma, ages 16–18 — university preparation

That is 13 years of schooling from Year 1 to Year 13 — one year more than most national systems counting from age 6. This offset is the most common source of confusion for families relocating internationally.

The 1 September Age Cutoff Rule

Almost all British-curriculum schools in Dubai use 1 September as the age cutoff date. The rule is simple: a child must have reached the required age by 1 September of that academic year to enter a given year group.

Example: Your child was born on 15 October 2019. On 1 September 2026 they are 6 years old — they have not yet turned 7. This means in September 2026 they enter Year 2, not Year 3. A child born on 1 August 2019 would be 7 on 1 September 2026 and enters Year 3.

American-curriculum and some IB schools may use different cutoff dates (often 1 August or 31 December). Always confirm with the specific school before submitting an application.

How Schools Determine Your Child's Year Group

At application, the school reviews:

  1. Passport or birth certificate — age checked against the 1 September cutoff
  2. Previous school reports or transcripts — last 1–2 years, confirming current grade and subject performance
  3. Previous curriculum overview — to understand what has been taught, especially in maths and English
  4. Placement test — common for Year 7 and above; maths and English assessed in person
  5. CAT4 assessment — required by most KHDA Outstanding schools; measures verbal, quantitative, non-verbal and spatial reasoning independently of curriculum

Knowing your child's academic level before the application — not just their grade, but how they compare to international benchmarks — means no surprises on assessment day. Borderline Situations: When Your Child Falls Between Two Year Groups

Children born in the summer months — particularly July and August — are most likely to be on the border between two year groups. This is well-known in British education as the "summer born" issue.

In a borderline case the school typically:

  • Checks the birth date precisely against the cutoff
  • Reviews previous school performance
  • May run an additional placement assessment or observation session
  • Asks parents about the child's social and emotional maturity

Moving a child into the higher year group requires stronger academic evidence. Placing them in the lower year group is a default that schools approve more readily. If you want the higher placement, a CAT4 score or an independent adaptive assessment showing above-average reasoning ability strengthens the case considerably.

Year 12 and 13: Special Considerations for Mid-Secondary Transfers

Families transferring a teenager who is already mid-secondary face more complexity. Year 12 is the entry point to A-Level or IB Diploma — both are two-year programmes with their own prerequisites:

  • Entry to Year 12 typically requires IGCSE-level achievement (or equivalent) in relevant subjects
  • For IB, prior exposure to academic English writing, the sciences and mathematics at the right level is assessed
  • A-Level subject choices must align with university ambitions; subject availability at the school matters

At this stage a consultant familiar with both the British/IB system and the student's current curriculum can map what has been covered, what gaps exist and which programme is the better fit.

Document Checklist for Year Group Determination

Passport copy — date of birth for age cutoff check
School reports / transcripts (last 2 years) — in English, or with a certified English translation
Vaccination record — KHDA standard requirement for all schools
Previous curriculum overview — helpful for Year 7+ to show maths and science coverage
Apostille-certified translation — required by some schools for documents from non-English-speaking countries; check the school's specific policy

Frequently Asked Questions

What age does a child start school in Dubai?

At most British-curriculum schools, a child must turn 5 by 1 September of the academic year to start Year 1. Reception (the year before Year 1) requires the child to turn 4 by 1 September. Always confirm the cutoff directly with the school.

My child is in Grade 4 — which year group will they join in Dubai?

Grade 4 (ages 9–10) corresponds to Year 5 in the British/Dubai system. However, the exact placement depends on your child's birth date relative to 1 September. The school will confirm once they have the passport and previous reports.

What happens if my child is borderline between two year groups?

The school reviews birth date, previous reports and may run a placement test or CAT4. Moving up a year group requires stronger evidence; the lower placement is easier to approve. An independent CAT4 or adaptive assessment result showing above-average reasoning ability strengthens the case for the higher group.

Does Dubai have Year 12 and 13?

Yes. Most British-curriculum Dubai schools offer Year 12 and Year 13, corresponding to A-Level or IB Diploma programmes — the equivalent of the final two years before university.

What documents does the school need to determine year group?

Passport, last two years of school reports in English (or with certified translation), vaccination record, and optionally a curriculum overview. Some schools require apostille-certified translations for documents from non-English-speaking countries.
