What Does "International" Mean? The Complete Guide to International Schools

Short Answer

"International" means "uluslararası" — multinational or cross-border. In education, an international school is a private institution that operates independently of any single national curriculum, offering globally recognised programmes such as IB, British A-Level or American AP to students from 20+ nationalities. Graduates can apply to universities worldwide.

What Is an International School?

An international school is a private school that delivers a globally recognised curriculum — such as IB, British IGCSE/A-Level, or American AP — independent of any single national education system. Three features define them: a multinational student body (typically 20–60 nationalities), English as the primary language of instruction, and global university recognition for graduates.

How Are International Schools Regulated?

Quality oversight varies by country, and knowing the difference matters:

  • Dubai: KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority) — Outstanding / Very Good / Good / Acceptable / Weak
  • UK: Ofsted — Outstanding / Good / Requires Improvement / Inadequate
  • Global: CIS (Council of International Schools) and WASC accreditations — available to international schools worldwide

KHDA and Ofsted reports are publicly accessible. Always read the official inspection report before choosing a school — not just what the admissions team tells you.

Can Turkish Children Succeed in International Schools?

Yes — consistently so. Turkish children typically excel due to strong maths and science foundations from the Turkish education system. This gives a concrete advantage particularly in IB and British curricula, where analytical rigour counts heavily.

The main challenge is not academic — it is social integration. The three areas to prioritise:

  • EAL support: For children still developing their English, the first 6–12 months are critical. Always ask: "Is there an EAL coordinator? How many hours of support per week?"
  • Extracurricular participation: Clubs, sport, music — finding social belonging comes before academic performance.
  • Turkish community connections: Connecting with other Turkish families at the school significantly speeds up social adaptation.

Five Steps to Choosing the Right International School

  1. 1
    Determine your target country — Which country will your child attend university in? UK/Australia → British. Global → IB. USA → American AP.
  2. 2
    Check the inspection rating — Read the KHDA, Ofsted or CIS report. These are publicly available and free. Trust independent inspection, not admissions brochures.
  3. 3
    Ask about EAL support — "Is there an EAL coordinator? How many hours per week?" Families who skip this question often struggle in year one.
  4. 4
    Visit the school — Check class sizes, library, sports and arts facilities in person. Brochure photos are not enough.
  5. 5
    Find the Turkish community — How many Turkish students are enrolled? Is there a Turkish parent network? Social belonging accelerates everything else.

The Scale and Growth of International Education

International education is one of the world's fastest-growing sectors. As of 2024, the International Baccalaureate Organisation (IBO) authorises approximately 5,700 schools across 159 countries — up from 3,000 in 2010. The Council of International Schools (CIS) accredits over 1,200 institutions globally. This growth reflects structural demand: multinational mobility, the globalisation of university admissions, and parental awareness that national exam-based systems increasingly fail to develop the competencies required by leading employers and universities.

For Turkish families specifically, international education represents a hedge against two domestic risks: the TL's long-run devaluation (which erodes the relative value of Turkish-system qualifications in global markets) and the Turkish education system's persistently low PISA rankings. Turkey's 2022 PISA scores placed it 40th out of 81 OECD and partner economies in mathematics — a result that has remained essentially flat for two consecutive cycles. Against this backdrop, the IB Diploma's recognition by over 5,700 schools and universities across 159 countries provides Turkish students with a qualification whose value is genuinely global and not subject to exchange rate or geopolitical risk.

University Outcomes: What the Data Shows

IBO research consistently shows that IB Diploma holders are admitted to selective universities at higher rates than the general applicant pool. A 2022 IBO study found that IB students in the United States were admitted to highly selective universities (those accepting fewer than 25% of applicants) at nearly twice the rate of non-IB applicants. In the UK, a disproportionate share of Oxford and Cambridge entrants hold IB Diplomas relative to A-Level holders at the same grade equivalent. For families with global university ambitions, this is not an abstract consideration — it is a measurable admission advantage.

For Turkish families navigating this decision — whether from within Turkey, planning a relocation to Dubai, or already abroad — understanding these structural differences early is one of the most time-efficient investments available before a school search begins. The earlier families engage with the IB system's expectations, the more strategically a child can be positioned for long-term success within it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "international" mean?

"International" means "uluslararası" in Turkish — multinational or involving more than one country. It combines "inter" (between) and "national" (of a nation). In education, "international school" refers to a school that operates independently of any single national curriculum and serves students from multiple countries.

What is an international school?

An international school is a private institution operating independently of any single national curriculum, serving students from multiple countries, typically using globally recognised curricula such as IB, British IGCSE/A-Level, or American AP. The language of instruction is usually English and graduates can apply to universities worldwide.

What is the difference between an international school and a regular school?

Regular state schools follow a single country's national curriculum; international schools offer a global curriculum that provides pathways to universities in multiple countries. The student body is multinational, instruction is usually in English, and quality is assessed by independent accreditation bodies.

Can Turkish children succeed in international schools?

Yes. The Turkish education system provides a strong mathematics and science foundation that is a significant advantage in international curricula. The main challenge is social integration and language adaptation — both of which can be overcome with the right support.

Are international schools expensive?

Fees vary considerably by country and school. In Dubai: 12,000–100,000 AED per year. In the UK: 15,000–60,000 GBP. In Germany: state-supported options range from 0–5,000 EUR. There are options to fit a range of budgets.

What age is easiest to transition to an international school?

Ages 5–10 represent the easiest adaptation window. Language acquisition is fastest at these ages. A transition in secondary school (14+) is more challenging but entirely achievable with the right support.
