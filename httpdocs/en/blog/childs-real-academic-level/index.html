Why School Grades Don't Tell the Whole Story

Every September the same question resurfaces: "Is my child doing well?" The report card says 85, the teacher says "a hard worker" — but where do they actually stand? This is entirely the right question to ask, because school grades are a remarkably limited data point.

A school grade compares your child to the average of that particular class, in that particular school. A student scoring 95 in a local school might score 60 on an international curriculum. A student scoring 70 in a rigorous city-centre school might be in the top global percentile. The class average is a moving target; it cannot be fixed as a reference point.

Traditional tests also only measure rote learning and a raw count of correct versus incorrect answers. They cannot tell you whether a child genuinely understood a concept or made a lucky guess. Cognitive depth, problem-solving fluency and analytical reasoning — none of these appear anywhere on a report card.

OECD data: In PISA 2022, Turkey scored an average of 453 in mathematics; the OECD average was 472 and Singapore scored 575. Yet most Turkish students receive "satisfactory" school grades. There is a deep gap between what a report card shows and what international standards measure.

This gap leads to a very practical question: how do I find my child's real academic level? The answer lies in adaptive testing.

Three Ways to Find Your Child's Real Level

Parents typically turn to one of three approaches. Each has advantages — and significant limitations.

1. Private tutor's assessment

Useful, but subjective. A tutor estimates based on their own experience; there is no standardised measurement tool. No comparable data against global peers is produced.

2. School exams and report cards

As noted above, these are shaped by each school's own criteria. Standards vary from teacher to teacher and city to city, producing a reference point that cannot be reliably fixed.

3. PISA-standard adaptive testing

The most reliable method. The same measurement framework used in international assessments benchmarks your child's ability against a global norm rather than a local class average. Eduentry offers this free of charge.

What Is Eduentry? What Does 2PL IRT Technology Mean for Parents?

Eduentry is an AI-powered academic assessment platform built on Two-Parameter Item Response Theory (2PL IRT) — the same statistical framework used in PISA, SAT and international baccalaureate examinations. Setting aside the technical term, for parents it means this:

The test adapts to your child — it doesn't bore or overwhelm them. In traditional exams every student receives the same questions: some find it far too easy, others far too hard. In Eduentry, each question is selected based on the child's previous answer. A correct answer brings a slightly harder question; an incorrect answer brings a slightly easier one. Within 20–30 questions the system homes in on your child's true ability threshold and continues measuring from there.

This approach has two major advantages: first, your child never feels either "I can't do this" or "this is boring, I've given up"; second, the measurement is dramatically more precise. A 60-question Eduentry assessment produces more reliable results than a 200-question traditional exam.

What are the "2 parameters"? Every question is evaluated on two properties: difficulty (what proportion of students answer this correctly?) and discrimination (does this question genuinely distinguish higher-ability from lower-ability students?). Instead of simply counting right and wrong, the system measures at which difficulty level your child succeeds. This is something a school grade can never do.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Eduentry assessment really free?

Yes, completely free. No hidden fees, subscriptions or credit card details are required. Simply create a free account and start the test immediately.

What is an adaptive test and how does it differ from a regular exam?

In an adaptive test, each subsequent question is selected based on the student's previous answer. A correct answer brings a harder question; an incorrect answer brings an easier one. This allows the system to pinpoint your child's true ability level far more quickly and precisely than a fixed-format test, which gives everyone identical questions and only counts right and wrong answers.

What does 2PL IRT technology mean?

2PL IRT (Two-Parameter Item Response Theory) is a statistical model that evaluates every question on two dimensions: difficulty level and discriminating power. Used in PISA, SAT and IB assessments worldwide, it measures which difficulty level your child can consistently succeed at — far more reliably than a simple right/wrong count.

How long does the test take and what ages is it for?

The assessment is 60 adaptive questions and takes approximately 60–90 minutes. It is suitable for students aged 6–17. Your child can pause and resume at any time.

My child gets excellent school grades — do they still need this test?

Absolutely. School grades reflect performance relative to classmates in that specific school. A child scoring 95 locally might be in the global 40th percentile; a child scoring 70 in a rigorous international school might be in the top 15%. The adaptive test benchmarks against global norms — exactly what matters for international school admissions, scholarships and long-term academic planning.

Can the results help with 11+ or scholarship exams?

Yes. The verbal and non-verbal reasoning sections in particular measure the analytical thinking skills that 11+, scholarship and selective school assessments rely on. Results directly inform which areas to prioritise in preparation.
