How to Find Your Child's Real Academic Level

Free PISA and IB-standard adaptive test to discover where your child stands globally — not just in their classroom.

In brief: Your child's school grade only compares them to classmates in that one school. International schools in Dubai and the UK use different benchmarks — CAT4, IB standards, GL Assessment. This guide explains what those tests actually measure, the Turkey–PISA gap, and four concrete ways to find your child's real academic level.

Why School Grades Don't Tell the Whole Story

Every September the same question resurfaces: "Is my child doing well?" The report card says 85, the teacher says "a hard worker" — but where do they actually stand compared to international peers? This is exactly the right question, because school grades are a remarkably limited data point.

A school grade compares your child to the average of that particular class, in that particular school. A student scoring 95 in a local school might score 60 on an international curriculum. A student scoring 70 in a rigorous international school might be in the top global percentile. The class average is a moving target; it cannot be used as a fixed reference point.

PISA 2022 data: Turkey scored an average of 453 in mathematics; the OECD average was 472 and Singapore scored 575. Yet most Turkish students receive "satisfactory" school grades. There is a real gap between what a report card shows and what international standards measure.

How International Schools Actually Assess Children

When a family moves to Dubai or the UK, they discover that international schools don't rely on report cards at all during admissions. They use standardised assessments designed to cut through the noise of local grading systems.

The CAT4 Test

CAT4 (Cognitive Abilities Test 4) is the most widely used assessment in Dubai's KHDA-regulated schools. It is published by GL Assessment and measures four reasoning areas: verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, non-verbal reasoning and spatial ability. Crucially, it measures potential, not what has been taught — a child with no IB teaching can still perform well if they have strong underlying reasoning ability.

Dubai schools use CAT4 results to:

  • Place students into the correct year group
  • Identify children who may need additional learning support
  • Spot high-potential students whose school grades don't reflect their true ability
  • Set baseline targets for academic progress

IB and UK Curriculum Benchmarks

Schools running the International Baccalaureate (IB) or British curriculum (IGCSE, A-Level) also benchmark against global norms from Cambridge Assessment or the IB organisation. A child entering Year 7 is expected to be working at a certain level in English and mathematics — regardless of what their previous school's grading system said.

Four Ways to Find Your Child's Real Academic Level

There is no single definitive source, but combining two or three of these gives a clear picture:

1. Private tutor assessment

A tutor working internationally can estimate whether your child is at UK Year 7 level in maths, for example. This is useful for quick orientation but inherently subjective — it depends entirely on the tutor's experience and benchmarks, not standardised data.

2. Official Cambridge or GL Assessment practice tests

GL Assessment (CAT4 publisher) and Cambridge both offer official past papers and practice materials. Working through these gives a concrete sense of format and difficulty. The limitation: scoring requires knowing the marking criteria, and without a norm group comparison you don't know where your child sits relative to peers.

3. Free adaptive online assessment

Eduentry offers a free PISA-standard adaptive test covering mathematics, English, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning. The adaptive format means questions adjust to your child's level in real time, producing a global percentile and a detailed PDF report. It's a good starting point for families who want data before booking a consultant or preparing for a CAT4.

4. International education consultant

An experienced consultant has placed hundreds of children into Dubai and UK schools. They can interpret assessment results, identify which schools suit your child's profile and build a targeted preparation plan. This is the most efficient route if you are already close to a move date. Özlem's consulting sessions cover exactly this process.

Priority Areas for Closing the Gap

Based on where Turkish-curriculum students most often fall short when entering international schools:

  1. Non-verbal reasoning — rarely practised in Turkey; CAT4 heavily weights this area. It can be improved quickly with targeted practice.
  2. Academic English — not grammar rules, but reading comprehension, text analysis and written argumentation at IGCSE level.
  3. Mathematical word problems in English — Turkish students often know the maths but can't parse the English language in multi-step problems.
  4. Verbal reasoning — word relationships, analogies, inferences. Similar to non-verbal, it is almost never tested in Turkish schools.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my child's level sufficient for an international school in Dubai?

There is no single pass/fail threshold — it depends on the school and year group. What matters is understanding where the gaps are before the application. Schools like GEMS, Nord Anglia and Repton typically assess children on entry through a CAT4-style test or their own placement exam. Knowing your child's current reasoning and English level in advance gives you time to prepare.

What does a Turkish school grade mean by international standards?

Very little on its own. Turkish school grades measure performance against that specific classroom's average, using a curriculum that is more procedural and less analytical than IB or UK curricula. PISA data consistently shows Turkish average scores are 15–20 points below the OECD mean — meaning a student with top marks in Turkey may be working around the OECD average internationally, not at the top.

What is the CAT4 test and why do Dubai schools use it?

CAT4 (Cognitive Abilities Test 4) is a standardised reasoning assessment by GL Assessment, used by most KHDA-regulated schools in Dubai. It measures verbal, quantitative, non-verbal and spatial reasoning — the cognitive foundations of learning — rather than what has been taught. Schools use it because it predicts academic potential independent of which curriculum a child came from, making it fairer for international transfers.

What should I do before applying to an international school?

Start with a baseline assessment (adaptive test or practice CAT4 papers), identify gaps in the four key areas above, work on those gaps systematically — especially non-verbal reasoning and academic English — and then book a consultation to review results before submitting applications. Doing this 3–6 months before your intended start date gives enough preparation time.

My child gets excellent grades but struggles in standardised tests — why?

This is very common for students transitioning from Turkish-curriculum schools. Good school grades typically reflect rote learning and formula application — skills measured differently or not at all in CAT4-style assessments, which test reasoning from first principles. The gap is real but closable: targeted practice in verbal and non-verbal reasoning tasks usually improves CAT4-style performance significantly within a few weeks.
