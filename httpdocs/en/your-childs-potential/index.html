How Can I Understand My Child's Academic Potential?

Short answer: Understanding your child's true academic potential requires an objective measurement independent of school grades. EduEntry uses Item Response Theory (IRT) to measure your child's real proficiency in English, Mathematics, Verbal and Quantitative Reasoning, benchmarks them against international peers, and gives you a concrete, actionable profile.

Many parents come with the same question: "My child is working hard but nothing's changing. They're getting tutoring but grades aren't moving. Are we missing something?" The answer usually isn't a lack of capacity — it's the wrong starting point.

Every investment made without knowing which area to work on, and at which level, is a guess. Seeing the real potential removes the guesswork.

Why Is My Child Struggling? 3 Real Reasons

1. Working at the wrong level If a student is working with material far above or below their real level, no amount of effort produces results. For tutoring to be effective, the real level must first be known.
2. Strengths and weaknesses are lumped together A blanket judgement like "weak in Maths" doesn't tell you which sub-area to support. Quantitative reasoning might be strong while processing speed is weak — or vice versa. Without domain-by-domain measurement, the right intervention is impossible.
3. Potential isn't measured — only grades are tracked School grades assess the teacher's curriculum, not the child. Seeing where your child stands relative to international peers requires an independent, objective measurement.

The Same Science as PISA and WISC

Item Response Theory (IRT) — 2-Parameter Logistic Model EduEntry applies the same methodology used by internationally standardised assessments such as PISA, GCSE and WISC. Every question is defined by two parameters: difficulty (the ability level at which a child has a 50% chance of answering correctly) and discrimination (how well the question distinguishes children at that level).
Adaptive Assessment: 15 Questions = Precision of 100 After each answer, the system updates its ability estimate and selects the next question for maximum measurement precision. This delivers the equivalent accuracy of a fixed 100-question test in far less time — the child doesn't fatigue, and the data stays solid.
